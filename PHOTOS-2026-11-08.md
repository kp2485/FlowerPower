# Photographs: quality and sharing — 2026-11-08

What a photograph goes through in FlowerPower, after a pass over every step
from the camera to a friend's garden. Kyle asked for the best way to improve
photo sharing and photo quality; this is what was found and what changed.

## What was wrong

- **Every photograph was drawn from a 400-pixel thumbnail.** Enough for a
  garden tile, a third of what the detail sheet's full-width square draws on
  a 3× screen. The one place a player went to look at their picture was the
  one place it was soft.
- **A shared flower was JPEG-compressed twice.** The sender shrank it to
  1,400 pixels at quality 0.75; the recipient decoded it and ran the same
  shrink-and-encode again before keeping it. Passing a gift on added a third.
- **The sender's picture came from the thumbnail API** and was encoded on
  the main thread, which is why typing a name needed a 350 ms delay.
- **The share sheet showed a blank document icon** for a `.flower`.
- **Backups carried no photographs** (audit finding 41), so a colony moved
  to a new phone arrived with grey tiles and nothing to send.
- **The classifiers only looked at the centre square** of the frame.
- **The camera was the stock picker**: no macro lens for close subjects, no
  tap to focus, and a decoded image re-encoded to HEIC before Photos saw it.

## What changed

**`PhotoLoader`** (new) is the one place to ask for a picture. Callers say
how many pixels they are drawing into; it rounds up to a bucket, asks Photos
or the app's store for at least that, caches the decode, and — when Photos
has nothing by an identifier — looks in the app's store under that same
identifier. `PhotoThumbnail` measures itself with `onGeometryChange` and asks
for what it needs. The detail sheet's square opens `PhotoViewer`, the whole
frame with a pinch.

**`SharedImageStore.prepareForSharing`** now takes bytes, not a `UIImage`.
A JPEG already within the limits is copied pixel for pixel through
`CGImageDestinationCopyImageSource` with its metadata replaced by none and
its orientation written back, so a friend's flower is passed on exactly as
it arrived. Anything else is decoded upright at 2,048 pixels and encoded at
0.85, stepping down until it fits the 4 MB cap. The sender reads the
original file from Photos (`PhotoLibrary.originalData`) and does all of this
off the main actor, once; typing only rewrites the small JSON around it.
`ReceiveFlowerView` keeps the validated bytes as they came. Every share
sheet has a `SharePreview`: the flower's picture and name, or a symbol.

Measured on the simulator: the ice-plant photograph went out at 2,048 × 1,536
in 1.55 MB, 11.7 KB lighter than the stored file, with no GPS, device or
time left in it — only the Exif pixel dimensions, an ICC profile and a
resolution block.

**Backups carry photographs.** `SaveArchive.photographs` is an optional list
of `(identifier, jpeg)`, each re-encoded to 1,024 pixels at 0.8 by the same
path a share takes, capped per picture and in number, and dropped rather
than refusing the colony when wrong. The cap on an archive rises from 16 MB
to 64 MB; a backup is decoded off the main actor now. Settings has an
"Include photographs" toggle and the footer says what went in ("3 of its 3
photographs, 1.2 MB"). Restoring keeps each picture in the app's store under
the identifier its patch already carries, which the loader finds when this
phone's library does not — the engine never learns a picture moved. Verified
end to end on the simulator: export, open from Files, restore with the
original files hidden, and the garden drew all three from the backup.

**A second look for off-centre flowers.** `FeaturePrints.salientSquare` asks
Vision's objectness saliency where the subject is and frames a padded
square round it, no smaller than a third of the short edge and only when it
differs from the centre crop. `FlowerClassifier` runs BioCLIP on both and
keeps the finer-ranked placement, or the surer one at the same rank. Not
measured against a photo set — there is none in the repository — so the
rule is conservative: the second pass can only replace the first when it
reaches a finer rank or beats it at the same rank.

**A real camera** is on its own branch (`macro-camera`): an AVFoundation
screen using the triple or dual-wide virtual camera so iOS switches to the
macro lens, tap to focus, a square guide matching the classifiers' crop, and
the camera's own HEIF bytes written straight to Photos. The simulator has no
camera, so it needs Kyle's phone to be seen.

## Tests

- Package: `SaveArchivePhotographTests` — round trip, absence, dropping bad
  pictures, the count cap, export through the store.
- App (`FlowerPowerTests/PhotoPipelineTests.swift`): the lossless strip
  removes GPS, lens and device and keeps the pixels; a camera original comes
  down to 2,048 and loses its location; the byte limit is honoured; the
  archive size is smaller; loader buckets; downsampling covers a square;
  the salient-square geometry; the store fallback for a foreign identifier.

## Still open

- The saliency pass has no accuracy measurement. `docs/CLASSIFIER.md`
  describes the held-out set to build; until then the rule stays
  conservative.
- Photos' limited-access mode: a photograph the app cannot read is left out
  of a backup, and the footer says how many.
